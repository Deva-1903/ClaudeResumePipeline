Dear Gemini Engineering Team,

I am applying for the Software Engineering Intern role. The line in your posting that Gemini is regulated like a bank but run like a tech startup is exactly the kind of environment I want to be in, and the problems you described, securing customer funds, shaving microseconds off API response times, and getting code right the first time, map closely to the work I have already done.

Before grad school I spent two years as a backend engineer at Wysa, where I owned a multi-tenant NHS clinical backend serving 8+ UK clients and processing 10,000+ submissions a month. A large part of that job was security and reliability: I hardened authentication and rate limiting, implemented AWS KMS envelope encryption for sensitive data, and remediated 20+ VAPT findings, then supported the system in production by monitoring logs, diagnosing tenant-specific incidents, and shipping fixes. Working where a small mistake genuinely costs something taught me to treat code review, tests, and monitoring as the job rather than overhead, which is the mindset I would bring to systems guarding real customer funds.

I also care about performance and correctness under load. On a personal project I cut a Spark ETL pipeline's runtime about 25\% on a 1.4-billion-row dataset and pulled its tail-latency ratio from 2.14x down to 1.74x by profiling stages and removing shuffle and skew bottlenecks. On AgenticSearch, a provenance-first discovery service, I hardened the pipeline with 150+ automated tests and provider fallback. And the project I am proudest of is a Stripe-based payments and membership platform I built solo that has handled real money for a small business and stayed live for 3+ years, so financial systems where reliability is non-negotiable are familiar ground.

Crypto and Web3 are areas I am still early in, but the underlying mission, building open, secure financial infrastructure that more people can trust, is exactly what pulls me toward Gemini. I learn fastest when I am a little out of my depth, and I would be glad to be challenged to raise the bar on a team like yours in your New York office.

Thank you for your time and consideration.
