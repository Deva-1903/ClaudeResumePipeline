Dear iHerb Hiring Team,

I want to work on systems where a small mistake actually costs something. Warehouse execution and control software that keeps high-volume, mission-critical fulfillment moving in real time is exactly that kind of system, and it is the kind of work I did for two years before grad school. At Wysa, I engineered a multi-tenant NHS eTriage backend serving 8+ UK clinical clients and processing 10,000+ monthly triage submissions in a workflow where downtime directly affected patient access to care. Reliability was the job: I added integration tests to guard against regressions, hardened authentication and rate limiting, implemented AWS KMS encryption for clinical data, remediated 20+ VAPT findings, and monitored production logs during releases so tenant-specific incidents were diagnosed and fixed at the root. The rest of my work maps closely to the patterns this role names: I have built asynchronous, queue-backed generation with rate limits in production (Cario), a DB-persisted job queue with orphaned-job recovery for long-running work (UMass Culture \& Morality Lab, current role), a distributed event-driven service with durable multi-step workflows (cf-ai-research-scout), and a high-volume data pipeline over roughly 1.4 billion rows (Spark).

I will be direct about the one gap you will notice: my professional stack has been Node.js, TypeScript, and Python rather than C\# and .NET. I do not consider a language the hard part of this job, and my track record backs that up. I led a JavaScript-to-TypeScript migration across Cario's core repositories, picked up C++ performance engineering (AVX vectorization, OpenMP) in graduate coursework, and have Java from coursework as well. The concepts your posting emphasizes, REST APIs, event-driven messaging, caching, concurrency, and object-oriented design, are language-portable, and I learn fastest when I am a little out of my depth. I would expect to be writing productive C\# within my first weeks, with code reviews from your senior engineers to keep me idiomatic.

The chance to build IoT and warehouse execution systems that 180+ countries' worth of orders flow through, while learning the .NET stack from a team that runs it at enterprise grade, is exactly the internship I am looking for. Thank you for your consideration.
