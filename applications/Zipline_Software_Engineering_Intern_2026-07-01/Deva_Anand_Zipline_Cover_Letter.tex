Dear Hiring Team,

The line in your description that stuck with me is that the drone is only 15\% of what you have built. The other 85\% is software that has to move blood, vaccines, and medicine to real healthcare systems without failing. I spent two years building healthcare software where reliability was the whole point, so I am applying for the Software Engineering Internship on your consumer apps team because a mission where a software bug has real-world consequences is exactly the kind of work I want to do.

Before grad school, at Wysa, I took technical ownership of a multi-tenant NHS clinical backend serving 8+ UK clinical clients from design through deployment and iteration, and I was the primary point of contact when something broke in production. I care about correctness under load because I have lived the version where it matters. I also like owning products end to end: I built Underdogs Fitness, a customer-facing gym-management platform, solo from scratch, and it has been running in production for 3+ years, quietly serving real members and later extended to a second branch. Shipping something people actually depend on, and then keeping it alive, taught me more about product thinking than any single feature ever did.

On the technical side, I work across the stack: React and Electron front ends, Python (FastAPI) and Node.js backends, and REST APIs designed in close collaboration with frontend developers on shared contracts. I am comfortable being handed an unfamiliar stack and getting productive fast, so whether the work is in the React Native mobile codebase or in Python and Go backend services, I am confident I can ramp quickly and start contributing features. I care about the details of a user-facing experience, keeping interfaces fast and responsive even when there is a lot happening underneath, the way I did building an offline real-time desktop app under hackathon time pressure.

What draws me to Zipline specifically is the combination you describe: sculpting from first principles and doing hard things at speed. I default to first principles, if I cannot rebuild a piece of a system myself I do not trust that I understand it, and I am genuinely motivated by work that gives people something concrete, like faster access to medicine. I would love to build features that reach people that directly.

Thank you for your time and consideration.
